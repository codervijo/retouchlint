## California — AB 723 → Bus. & Prof. Code § 10140.8 (sales) — in force since 2026-01-01

Checked 2026-09-30 (leginfo); quoted in full on MLSListings' AB 723 article.
(a)(1) statement that the image has been altered + link to a publicly accessible website,
URL or QR code that includes and clearly identifies the original; reasonably conspicuous,
on or adjacent to the image; must say the unaltered images can be accessed at the link.
(a)(2) on a website the licensee controls: include the unaltered versions, or a link.
(b)(1) definition (fixtures … neighboring properties); (b)(2) exclusions (lighting,
sharpening, white balance, color correction, angle, straightening, cropping, exposure).

## California — AB 2025 (Pellerin), Chapter 572, Statutes of 2026 — rentals

Sources: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billStatusClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB2025
and …/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB2025 (read via treg.web.extract — leginfo
blocks plain curl).

- Status: "Inactive Bill - Chaptered"; Chaptered 09/27/26; "Approved by Governor September
  27, 2026. Filed with Secretary of State September 27, 2026." Type: "Non-Urgency".
  → Effective 2027-01-01 (non-urgency statute enacted in 2026; Cal. Const. art. IV § 8(c)).
- **Amends § 10140.8:** (a)(1) now reads "a link, URL, or QR code to a publicly accessible
  internet website that includes, and clearly identifies, the original, unaltered image"
  and "can be accessed via the link, URL, or QR code". New **(c)**: "This section does not
  apply to an advertisement or other promotional material for a leasehold exceeding one
  year's duration." (Digest: Real Estate Law's "sale" definition includes a leasehold > 1
  year; AB 2025 moves those to the rental rule.)
- **Adds Civil Code § 1940.11 (rentals):**
  - (a)(1) "Capture device" = meaning in B&P § 22757.1 (not read; don't define further).
  - (a)(2)(A) "Digitally altered image": (i) "fully generated through artificial
    intelligence software by or at the direction of a person", or (ii) an existing image
    altered with photo-editing software or AI to add, remove or change elements (same list
    as § 10140.8(b)(1)). (B) same exclusions as § 10140.8(b)(2).
  - (b)(1) "A person who includes a digitally altered image in an advertisement or other
    promotional material for rental of real property shall include … a disclosure that the
    image has been altered. The disclosure shall be reasonably conspicuous and located on or
    adjacent to the image."
  - (b)(2)(A) the ad "shall include the unaltered version of the images … in the posting";
    may comply via "a link, URL, or QR code to a publicly accessible internet website that
    includes, and clearly identifies, the original, unaltered image"; if so, the disclosure
    must say the unaltered images can be accessed via the link, URL or QR code.
  - (b)(2)(B) (A) "does not apply to an image, architectural rendering, or artistic
    rendering that was not originally created through the use of a capture device."
  - (c) a real estate licensee who violates it "shall be deemed to have violated that
    person's licensing law." (d) doesn't alter false-advertising law.
  - Applies to "a person" — not limited to licensees.

## New York — A11635 (2025–2026 term) — in committee

Source: https://nyassembly.gov/leg/?default_fld=&leg_video=&bn=A11635&term=2025&Summary=Y&Actions=Y&Text=Y&Memo=Y

- Introduced July 29, 2026 by Committee on Rules (at request of M. of A. Rosenthal);
  "07/29/2026 referred to housing". No same-as Senate bill listed.
- Adds Real Property Law § 443-b. "Digitally altered image": still photo, created by or at
  the behest of "the real estate broker, seller, property owner or property manager",
  altered with photo-editing software or AI to add, remove or change elements of the
  property "including, but not limited to, furniture, appliances, flooring, and walls";
  excludes lighting, sharpening, white balance, color correction, angle, straightening,
  cropping, exposure, other common adjustments "that do not change the conditions of the
  real property". "AI-generated image": still or **video** image created "in whole or in part
  by generative artificial intelligence".
- 2(a) broker, seller, property owner or property manager including a digitally altered or
  AI-generated image in an ad for "sale or lease" → statement that the image has been
  altered, "conspicuously located on or adjacent to each altered image".
- 2(b) if posted on a website → include the unaltered versions; "may comply … by including
  a link to the original image".
- Effective "on the sixtieth day after it shall have become a law."

## Washington, DC — B26-0858, "Restricting Editing and Alterations in Listing Photos Amendment Act of 2026" — introduced

Sources: https://lims.dccouncil.gov/Legislation/B26-0858 ; introduced text
https://lims.dccouncil.gov/downloads/LIMS/62714/Introduction/B26-0858-Introduction.pdf
(status detail from billtrack50 — secondary: "In Committee 10/20/2026").

- Statement of introduction, October 9, 2026, CM Christina Henderson with CMs Frumin,
  Nadeau, Bonds, White, Lewis George, Parker. Covers listing agents, realtors and property
  managers; properties "offered for lease or sale in the District". Mentions "New York City
  is considering rulemaking for similar restrictions" (attribute to the statement only).
- Adds D.C. Code § 28-3901(16) definition: same element list as AB 723; excludes common
  adjustments "that do not **materially** change the representation of the real property".
- Adds § 28-3904(nn) (Consumer Protection Procedures Act unlawful trade practice): "Fail to
  include a clear and conspicuous disclosure in an advertisement or listing for real
  property, whether available for rent or sale, that the advertisement includes a digitally
  altered image. The disclosure shall include a **written description of material
  differences** between any digitally altered image and the real property's actual
  condition, features, and amenities. The advertisement or listing shall include a publicly
  accessible and unedited version of any digitally altered image."
- Amends § 47-2853.197(39): licensee false/misleading advertising "including by violating §
  28-3904(nn)".
- Effective after Mayor's approval and a 30-day congressional review period.
